# Project 001 — Marine Underwater Adhesion 

## Investigating how marine mussels achieve strong adhesion in wet environments and what their biological strategies can teach us about biomimetic materials.

**Project:** Pelagica Ocean Science
**Pillar I:** Marine Research
**Pillar II:** Biomimicry Design
**Status:** Literature research
**Started:** 2026

---

## 1. Research Question

> **How do marine mussels achieve strong adhesion in wet environments, and which biological principles could inform the design of improved biomimetic underwater adhesives?**

---

## 2. Background

Adhesion in wet environments presents a significant materials challenge. Water can interfere with interactions between an adhesive and a surface, making it difficult to achieve strong and durable attachment.

Marine mussels provide a particularly interesting biological system because they maintain attachment to submerged surfaces despite continuous exposure to seawater, waves, currents, and mechanical forces.

Mussels attach themselves using specialized structures known as **byssal threads**, which contain adhesive and structural components that allow the animal to remain attached to surfaces.

This project investigates the biological mechanisms underlying this process and explores whether these mechanisms reveal useful principles for biomimetic material design.

---

## 3. Objectives

### Biological Research

* Understand how mussels attach to submerged surfaces.
* Investigate the structure and composition of byssal threads.
* Identify the molecules, proteins, and chemical interactions involved in adhesion.
* Examine how mussel adhesion functions under environmental conditions such as seawater, mechanical stress, and changing surfaces.
* Identify adaptations that contribute to adhesion performance.

### Biomimicry Design

* Identify the fundamental biological principles responsible for mussel adhesion.
* Examine how these principles have already been translated into synthetic materials.
* Identify limitations in existing biomimetic approaches.
* Identify potential opportunities for new biomimetic adhesive designs.
* Develop research-backed hypotheses for future investigation.

## 5. Research Questions

### Biology

1. How do mussels attach to surfaces?
2. What is the structure of a byssal thread?
3. Which proteins are involved in adhesion?
4. How are these proteins organized?
5. How does adhesion occur at the mussel–surface interface?
6. How does the mussel maintain adhesion in seawater?
7. How does the system respond to mechanical stress?
8. How does adhesion vary between mussel species and environments?

### Chemistry & Materials Science

9. Which chemical interactions contribute to adhesion?
10. What role does water play at the adhesive interface?
11. What role do metal ions and other environmental components play?
12. How do the mechanical and chemical properties of the adhesive work together?

### Biomimicry

13. Which biological principles are most important for the adhesive's performance?
14. Which of these principles have already been reproduced synthetically?
15. What limitations exist in current biomimetic mussel-inspired adhesives?
16. Are there biological mechanisms that remain underused or poorly translated?
17. Could combinations of biological strategies provide new design opportunities?
